## Overview

The Building Upgrade System is the sanctuary's synergy layer: it is where a structure the player has already built in Defense Building becomes eligible for a further, permanent improvement — but only once the player's progress across the *rest* of the sanctuary has independently produced the specific things that upgrade calls for. Technically, it is a second, orthogonal progression surface layered on top of Defense Building's existing tech-tree/tier-unlock system, not a replacement for it: Defense Building still owns *whether a structure exists at all* (its tech gate, its tier sequence, its gold/material cost); this system owns *whether an already-built structure can be made better*, and it asks a different kind of question to unlock that — not "did you pay the price," but "have you also built this other structure, crafted this specific piece of equipment, unlocked this specific tarot card, and drawn this specific creature into your menagerie." Every `BuildingUpgradeResource` recipe names 1–4 of those four requirement categories explicitly, by identifier, and every named requirement must be independently satisfied — a mixed-currency recipe whose ingredients are read from other systems' own persistent state rather than consumed from Inventory. Like every other Defense Building output, an upgrade is a permanent, Sanctuary-only, EventBus-decoupled effect: it fires `structure_upgraded`, and whichever system owns that structure's actual mechanical effect (Menagerie for Habitat Expansion, a future consumer for Watchtower) is the one that reacts to it, exactly mirroring `sanctuary_structure_built`'s existing decoupling. Player-facing, it is the payoff moment for having played broadly rather than deeply in any one system: the sanctuary occasionally hands you an upgrade you could not have rushed toward on purpose, because it was waiting on a tarot card, a tamed creature, and a piece of gear you crafted for entirely unrelated reasons, to all be true on the same save file at once.

## Player Fantasy

> No other system in the sanctuary asks you to have already done four unrelated things right. This one does — and then it tells you that you have.
>
> Every other purchase in the sanctuary is a straight line: you have the gold, you have the materials, you spend them, you get the thing. This is not that. This is the sanctuary looking back across everything else you have built — a structure raised in Defense Building, a piece of gear you forged for a completely different reason, a tarot card that has sat in your unlocked pool since a mission three sessions ago, a creature you tamed because you liked it, not because you were farming toward anything — and telling you, unprompted, that those four unrelated choices have quietly added up to a fifth thing you didn't know you were building. You didn't grind for this upgrade. You *arrived* at it, sideways, by playing the rest of the game the way you actually wanted to play it.
>
> That is the specific and singular feeling this system exists to produce: the moment the game proves, mechanically, that nothing you did was wasted, even the parts that felt disconnected. The tarot card you unlocked for its wave-modifier, not for this. The creature you tamed for the menagerie's own sake. The gear you forged for the hero to wear. None of them were spent toward this upgrade — they were never consumed, never staked, never sacrificed for it. They just had to *exist*, all at once, in the same save. The upgrade doesn't take them from you to grant itself. It simply notices they're all true, and hands you the door they were quietly holding open the whole time.
>
> And because the requirement is a specific combination, not a threshold you can grind linearly within any single system, a player who drives one system deep and ignores the rest entirely will watch these upgrades sit locked no matter how much gold or how many missions they throw at them — not because they did anything wrong, but because this system's entire point is to reward breadth. It is the sanctuary's proof that "everything connects" is not just a description of how the systems are wired to each other, but a description of what the player is actually rewarded for doing: playing the whole game, not just the part of it they're best at.
>
> **Honest caveat** (design review, 2026-08-12): requirements are checked, never consumed (Rule 5) — a player who already knows exactly what a specific upgrade wants *can* deliberately assemble it piece by piece (craft the one item, tame the one creature) and cash it in on purpose. That's not a loophole to close; it's the same system serving a second, more deliberate kind of player without contradiction — engaging a system on purpose to satisfy a requirement is still playing that system, not skipping it. What the fantasy actually promises is narrower and still true: breadth across the sanctuary is *necessary* (a player who only ever touches one system will never see these fire), even though it stops being *sufficient proof of surprise* once a player has read enough of their own save file to go hunting for a specific combination on purpose.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

**Upgrade Recipe Model**

1. A `BuildingUpgradeResource` defines one purchasable upgrade:

   | Field | Type | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | `upgrade_id` | StringName | Unique identifier |
   | `target_structure_id` | StringName | The Defense Building `structure_id` (`defense-building.md` Rule 5's schema) this upgrade applies to. Must already be `BUILT` for the upgrade to even appear (Rule 3). |
   | `prerequisite_upgrade_id` | StringName | The prior upgrade in this structure's own upgrade chain, or `""` if this is the structure's first available upgrade. Mirrors `defense-building.md` Rule 5's `prerequisite_structure_id` sequential-tier convention exactly — a structure may have more than one upgrade over time, always purchased in order. |
   | `requirements` | `Dictionary[String, StringName]` | 1–4 entries. Keys are drawn from `VALID_REQUIREMENT_TYPES` (`"building"`, `"equipment"`, `"tarot"`, `"creature"`); each value is the specific identifier required for that category (a `structure_id`, an `item_id`, a `card_id`, or a `species_id`, respectively). See Rule 2 for how each category resolves. |
   | `gold_cost` | int | Paid on confirm, same debit mechanism as Defense Building's own tech/structure costs (`resources_changed(-cost, "structure_upgrade")`) |
   | `material_cost` | `Dictionary[StringName, int]` | Rarity-keyed (`Common`/`Uncommon`/`Rare`), matching `crafting.md` Formula 1 / `defense-building.md` Formula 1's own vocabulary |
   | `effect_description` | String | Free text, content-authoring only — mirrors `defense-building.md` Rule 3's `gates` field. The mechanical effect itself is owned by whichever system consumes `structure_upgraded` (Rule 6), not by this Resource. |

1a. **Chain length is capped at 3** (added 2026-08-12 design review): a `target_structure_id`'s `prerequisite_upgrade_id` chain may contain at most 3 upgrades (`upgrade_tier` 1–3), matching Formula 1's own `upgrade_tier_multiplier` lookup table, which is only defined for those 3 values. A 4th upgrade in the same chain has no defined cost — enforced, not just implied, by `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate()` (Rule 1b).

1b. **Content validation, extended 2026-08-12 design review**: `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate() -> Array[String]` already existed pre-review (mirroring `crafting.md`'s `RecipeResource.validate()` — the `Array[String]`-of-error-messages convention, not `TechNodeResource.validate()`'s bool+`push_error()` one) and already rejects, at load time: `upgrade_id`/`target_structure_id` empty, `target_structure_id` referencing an unrecognized structure, and an unrecognized `requirements` key or empty requirement value. This pass **extends** that same method with 2 checks it didn't previously have: a `prerequisite_upgrade_id` referencing a different `target_structure_id` (Edge Cases), and a same-`target_structure_id` chain that either cycles back to an already-visited `upgrade_id` or exceeds 3 upgrades (Edge Cases, Rule 1a) — both require cross-Resource sibling context beyond what a single-Resource `validate()` call sees, so the chain-walk itself likely needs a second pass over the full loaded set (e.g. in `GameDataRegistry._load_building_upgrades()`, which already runs after `_load_structures()` for the existing `target_structure_id` cross-reference) rather than fitting inside `validate()`'s own single-instance signature unchanged.

2. **Requirement resolution** — each `requirements` entry is checked independently and non-destructively (nothing is consumed by checking, or by a successful upgrade — see Rule 4), and each entry's satisfied/unmet state is tracked and shown individually, not collapsed into one pass/fail flag for the whole upgrade (Rule 2a, added 2026-08-12 design review — see AC-6):
   - `"building"` → `GDefenseBuilding.is_structure_built(structure_id)` (already existed, `defense-building.md` Rule 5's own API)
   - `"equipment"` → `Inventory.has_specific_item(item_id)` (implemented 2026-08-11, this system's own addition — `has_items()` only sums by `item_type`/quantity, not "do I own this exact `item_id`")
   - `"tarot"` → `GTarot.is_card_unlocked(card_id)` (the method itself already existed, `tarot.md`'s own discovery-ledger API) — **RESOLVED 2026-08-12 (ADR-0035)**: Tarot is now a real Autoload, reachable from Sanctuary UI like every other requirement category
   - `"creature"` → `Menagerie.has_creature_species(species_id)` (implemented 2026-08-11, this system's own addition — `get_all_creatures()` returns every owned instance; nothing previously answered "do I own at least one of species X" directly)

2a. **Per-requirement legibility (added 2026-08-12 design review)**: once an upgrade is `VISIBLE_LOCKED` or `PURCHASABLE` (i.e. its structure is built and the player can already see the entry — Rule 3's HIDDEN state is the only place pre-discovery mystery applies), every one of its `requirements` entries renders as its own row/chip, not a single collapsed lock icon — reusing Crafting's own established per-ingredient checklist convention (`crafting.md` Rule 4/Visual Requirements: each ingredient line shows its own satisfied/insufficient state, non-color-safe). Each chip shows: (a) its category (building/equipment/tarot/creature, via a distinct icon per category — colorblind-safe shape, not color-only, matching `defense-building.md`'s built/unbuilt distinction precedent), (b) the specific target's resolved display name (the structure/item/card/creature-species name, read from that target's own content resource — never just "a creature"), and (c) a satisfied (checkmark, filled) vs. unmet (lock glyph, non-color-safe) state, live-updating on the same EventBus re-evaluation Rule 4 already specifies. This is a deliberate, narrower promise than blind mystery: Rule 3's HIDDEN state is where "you didn't know you were building this" surprise lives (the upgrade doesn't exist on screen at all until its structure is built); once an entry is already visible, withholding which of its 1-4 requirements are met would only produce a dead-end screen, not preserve any part of the Player Fantasy.

3. **Visibility**: an upgrade is only ever shown to the player once `target_structure_id` is `BUILT` (Defense Building's own state, Rule 5 above) — an upgrade for a structure the player hasn't built yet is not listed at all, not merely disabled. This mirrors Crafting's own discovery-vs-craftability split (`crafting.md` Rule 4) in spirit, but is simpler: there is no separate one-way "discovery" ledger here, because the gate (`is_structure_built`) is itself already a permanent, one-way Defense Building state — nothing about visibility needs its own ledger.

4. **Purchasability**: once visible, an upgrade is purchasable the instant **all** of its `requirements` entries AND both costs (Rule 1) are simultaneously satisfied. Every requirement re-evaluates live on every relevant EventBus signal (Rule 6) — exactly like Crafting's own craftability flicker (`crafting.md` Rule 4) — since a creature can leave the menagerie, a card is never un-unlocked but an equipment item *can* be discarded/consumed elsewhere, temporarily revoking an otherwise-available upgrade.

5. **Confirmation is non-destructive to the requirement inputs**: purchasing an upgrade consumes `gold_cost` and `material_cost` only (Rule 1's cost fields). The `requirements` entries (Rule 2) are checked, not spent — the built structure stays built, the equipment stays in Inventory, the tarot card stays unlocked, the creature stays in the menagerie. This is a deliberate design choice (Player Fantasy: "none of them were spent toward this upgrade") distinguishing this system from Crafting's consumption-based recipe model, even though the two share the same `RecipeResource`-adjacent shape.

6. **Effect ownership**: on successful purchase, this system emits `structure_upgraded(structure_id, upgrade_id)` (merged into `event-bus.md`'s catalogue 2026-08-11) and does nothing further. It does not itself grant capacity, stats, or any other mechanical effect — exactly like `defense-building.md`'s own `sanctuary_structure_built` for Habitat Expansion, whose slot-capacity effect is owned entirely by `menagerie.md`, not by Defense Building. Which system consumes `structure_upgraded` for which `target_structure_id`/`upgrade_id` is content-authoring-level scaffolding today (Rule 7), not yet mechanically wired for either named structure family.

6a. **Notification feedback, added 2026-08-12 design review** (closes the gap between this GDD's own Player Fantasy and its original silence on how that fantasy is actually delivered):
    - The `VISIBLE_LOCKED` → `PURCHASABLE` transition (Rule 4) fires a **Sanctuary UI Connection Badge** on the Equipment Forge tab — reusing the existing mechanism verbatim (`sanctuary-ui.md` Core Rule 2: "purely EventBus-driven... a small live-updating badge... clears immediately the instant its room is entered"), not inventing a new notification channel. This is the actual delivery mechanism for "the sanctuary tells you, unprompted": the player does not need to have the Building Upgrade tab open, or even be checking for it, for the badge to appear the moment the last requirement or cost falls into place.
    - Purchase itself (`PURCHASABLE` → `PURCHASED`) plays a short confirmatory chime — reusing `defense-building.md`'s existing "Standard tech-node unlock" cue verbatim, for the same reason: gold and materials are genuinely spent (Rule 5) even though no mechanical effect is wired yet for either target structure family (Rule 7), so the moment of spend needs its own feedback beat independent of whatever downstream effect eventually exists.
    - Both reuse existing, already-specified project conventions — this rule adds no new VFX/audio design, only wires this system into the two closest-fitting ones that already exist.

7. **Content scaffolding, honestly flagged**: this GDD does not yet define what a Habitat Expansion or Watchtower upgrade actually *does* mechanically — mirroring `defense-building.md`'s own already-accepted "Watchtower's gameplay effect is deliberately undefined scaffolding" framing for the exact same reason (no consuming system has specified what it wants yet). `effect_description` (Rule 1) exists precisely so upgrade content can be authored and reviewed before the mechanical hookup exists, the same pattern Defense Building itself already uses for Watchtower.

8. **One-time, permanent, never reset**: like every Defense Building output, a purchased `upgrade_id` is permanent for the remainder of the save — never lost on mission defeat (`game-concept.md`'s standing rule for Sanctuary progression), never re-purchasable, never reversible.

### States and Transitions

| State | Meaning | UI | Transitions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HIDDEN` (implicit, default) | `target_structure_id` not yet `BUILT` | Not shown at all | `target_structure_id` becomes `BUILT` → `VISIBLE_LOCKED` |
| `VISIBLE_LOCKED` | Structure built, but ≥1 `requirements` entry unsatisfied or either cost unaffordable | Per-requirement chip row (Rule 2a) — non-color-safe lock cue per unmet entry (reuses Crafting's own "!"-badge-plus-lock-icon vocabulary, `crafting.md` Visual/Audio Requirements), checkmark per satisfied entry | All requirements + both costs satisfied → `PURCHASABLE`; any previously-met requirement becomes unmet again (Rule 4) → stays/returns to `VISIBLE_LOCKED` |
| `PURCHASABLE` | All gates and costs satisfied | Filled/actionable confirm button, matching Crafting's filled-vs-outline convention. **The `VISIBLE_LOCKED` → `PURCHASABLE` transition itself fires a Sanctuary UI Connection Badge on the Equipment Forge tab (Rule 6a)** — this is the "unprompted" moment the Player Fantasy is built on: the player does not have to be looking at this tab, or even know the upgrade exists yet in practice, for the sanctuary to flag that something just opened up | Player confirms → `PURCHASED` (terminal) |
| `PURCHASED` (terminal, save-persisted) | Upgrade bought | Shown as owned; a short confirmatory chime plays on confirm (Rule 6a, mirrors `defense-building.md`'s own "Standard tech-node unlock" cue) so spending gold+materials always has an immediate feedback beat, independent of whether any consuming system has a mechanical effect wired yet (Rule 7); `prerequisite_upgrade_id`-gated next upgrade (if any) becomes checkable | None — permanent |

## Formulas

**Formula 1 — Upgrade Cost** (deliberately reuses `defense-building.md` Formula 1's exact shape, since an upgrade's cost has the same "front-loaded scale by position in a chain" character as a tech node's):

```
gold_cost(upgrade_tier) = round(base_upgrade_gold × upgrade_tier_multiplier(upgrade_tier))
material_qty(rarity, upgrade_tier) = round(base_qty[rarity] × upgrade_tier_multiplier(upgrade_tier))
```

| Variable | Type | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `upgrade_tier` | int | 1–3 | This upgrade's position within its own `target_structure_id`'s upgrade chain (`prerequisite_upgrade_id` sequence), root = 1 |
| `base_upgrade_gold` | int | 40–100 (default 60) | Gold baseline at `upgrade_tier=1` — set slightly above `defense-building.md`'s `base_tech_gold` (30–80/default 50) since an upgrade's requirements (Formula 2) already gate access; the gold cost itself doesn't need to carry as much of the pacing burden |
| `upgrade_tier_multiplier(upgrade_tier)` | float | {1.0, 1.8, 3.0} | Identical constant to `defense-building.md`'s `node_tier_multiplier` — reused deliberately, not reinvented, per this project's own established "don't fork a scaling curve without a mechanical reason" precedent (see `crafting.md` Formula 3's explicit refusal to fork `tower.md`'s `TIER_DPS_MULTIPLIER`) |
| `base_qty[rarity]` | int | Common=12, Uncommon=6, Rare=3 | Baseline at `upgrade_tier=1`, slightly below `defense-building.md`'s own tech-node baseline (15/8/4) for the same reason as `base_upgrade_gold` above |

Worked example: `upgrade_tier=1` → `gold_cost = round(60 × 1.0) = 60`, `material_qty(Common, 1) = 12`. `upgrade_tier=2` → `gold_cost = round(60 × 1.8) = 108`, `material_qty(Common, 2) = round(12 × 1.8) = 22`.

**Formula 2 — Requirement Count vs. Upgrade Tier** (a content-authoring guideline, not a hard-enforced rule): later `upgrade_tier` entries in a chain should name strictly more `requirements` categories than earlier ones — e.g. `upgrade_tier=1` might name 1–2 categories, `upgrade_tier=3` should name 3–4. This keeps the "playing broadly pays off" Player Fantasy intact as chains deepen, rather than letting a late-tier upgrade be gated by cost alone. Not automatable as an Acceptance Criterion (it's a content-quality guideline, matching `crafting.md`'s own precedent for its "Default sort" UI guidance being descriptive, not load-bearing).

## Edge Cases

- **Structure sold/removed after an upgrade targeting it is `PURCHASED`**: out of scope — `defense-building.md` has no "remove a built structure" mechanic today (structures, like tech nodes, are permanent once built). If that ever changes, this system's own permanence rule (Core Rule 8) means the upgrade stays `PURCHASED` regardless; re-triggering `structure_upgraded` is not this system's concern.
- **A `requirements` entry references an unknown/malformed `structure_id`/`item_id`/`card_id`/`species_id`** (content-authoring error): the resolving query (Rule 2) returns `false` for an unrecognized identifier by construction (each upstream system's own query already handles "not found" as `false`, not an error) — the upgrade simply never becomes `PURCHASABLE`. Load-time validation (mirroring `crafting.md`'s own `RecipeResource.validate()` pattern) should still flag this as a content error, not rely on the silent runtime `false`.
- **Two upgrades in the same chain share a `requirements` entry** (e.g., both need the same tarot card): fully supported — Rule 2's checks are read-only and non-destructive (Rule 5), so any number of upgrades (for the same or different structures) can simultaneously depend on the same underlying tarot card/creature/equipment/building with no conflict.
- **The gating equipment item is discarded/consumed by another system after the upgrade's `requirements` were all momentarily true, but before the player confirms**: the upgrade reverts to `VISIBLE_LOCKED` (Rule 4's live re-evaluation) — matches Crafting's own stale-UI defense (`crafting.md` Edge Cases, "if materials are insufficient at confirm-time"); the confirm action must re-validate all `requirements` at the moment of confirmation, not trust a cached "was purchasable" snapshot.
- **`prerequisite_upgrade_id` points to an upgrade for a *different* `target_structure_id`**: a content-authoring error — every upgrade's chain must stay within its own `target_structure_id` (Rule 1's own field definition scopes `prerequisite_upgrade_id` to "the prior upgrade in this structure's own upgrade chain"). Rejected by `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate()` (Rule 1b, added 2026-08-12), same load-time mechanism `crafting.md`'s `RecipeResource.validate()` already establishes for this project — not aspirational prose, an actual pre-ship gate.
- **A same-`target_structure_id` `prerequisite_upgrade_id` chain forms a cycle** (e.g. A's prerequisite is B, B's prerequisite is A) — added 2026-08-12 design review: without a guard, AC-7's own rule ("B is not `PURCHASABLE` while A has not been `PURCHASED`") would deadlock every upgrade in the cycle permanently in `VISIBLE_LOCKED`, regardless of requirements/cost. Rejected by the same `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate()` pass (Rule 1b) via a chain-walk from each upgrade to its root, per-`target_structure_id`: any chain that revisits an `upgrade_id` already seen in its own walk is a content error, mirroring `defense-building.md` Rule 6's own tech-node prerequisite-chain validation (which has the identical cycle risk and the identical walk-based guard).

## Dependencies

- **Defense Building** — provides `is_structure_built()` (visibility gate + `"building"` requirement type) and owns every `target_structure_id` this system upgrades
- **Inventory** — provides `has_specific_item()` for `"equipment"` requirements (implemented 2026-08-11)
- **Tarot** — provides `is_card_unlocked()` for `"tarot"` requirements
- **Menagerie** — provides `has_creature_species()` for `"creature"` requirements (implemented 2026-08-11)
- **Equipment Forge** — hosts this system's own screen (the Forge's "Upgrade Buildings" tab); `restore_order=33` must sequence after Equipment Forge's `32`
- **Resource Economy** — indirect; this system's gold/material costs are paid from Resource Economy's own accumulated currency, no direct interface
- **EventBus** — emits `structure_upgraded` (merged into `event-bus.md` 2026-08-11), the new `building_upgrade_became_purchasable` (Rule 6a — proposed), and the existing `resources_changed` event
- **Save & Persistence** — `restore_order = 33`, implements `SaveRestorer`

**Bidirectional note**: `defense-building.md`, `equipment-forge.md` (source of crafted Equipment `item_id`s referenced by `"equipment"` requirements), `tarot.md`, and `menagerie.md` should each gain a one-line downstream-dependent note referencing this system, per this project's own bidirectional-dependency convention (`systems-index.md`'s enforcement precedent across every existing GDD pass).

## Tuning Knobs

| Knob | Default | Safe Range | Affects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base_upgrade_gold` | 60 | 40–100 | Gold cost floor at `upgrade_tier=1` (Formula 1) |
| `base_qty[Common/Uncommon/Rare]` | 12/6/3 | ±50% | Material cost floor at `upgrade_tier=1` (Formula 1) |
| `upgrade_tier_multiplier` | {1.0, 1.8, 3.0} | Shared with `defense-building.md` — do not tune independently | Cost scaling per upgrade tier (Formula 1) |
| Requirement categories per upgrade | 1–4 | Content-authoring choice per upgrade | How "broad" a given upgrade's Player Fantasy payoff is (Formula 2 guideline) |

## Acceptance Criteria

- **AC-1**: GIVEN `target_structure_id` is not yet `BUILT`, WHEN the Forge's Building Upgrade tab is viewed, THEN that upgrade is not listed at all (Rule 3).
- **AC-2**: GIVEN `target_structure_id` is `BUILT` and every `requirements` entry is satisfied but `gold_cost`/`material_cost` are not affordable, WHEN the tab is viewed, THEN the upgrade shows `VISIBLE_LOCKED` with a cost-insufficient cue, not `PURCHASABLE`.
- **AC-3**: GIVEN all `requirements` entries and both costs are satisfied, WHEN the player confirms, THEN `gold_cost`/`material_cost` are debited, the upgrade becomes `PURCHASED` permanently, and `structure_upgraded(structure_id, upgrade_id)` fires exactly once (verified via EventBus signal spy — this AC asserts the event fires, not any downstream mechanical effect, since no consumer is wired for either target structure family per Rule 7).
- **AC-4**: GIVEN a successful purchase per AC-3, WHEN the resulting Inventory/Tarot/Menagerie/Defense Building state is inspected, THEN none of the `requirements` inputs were consumed, removed, or otherwise mutated (Rule 5) — only the two cost fields changed.
- **AC-5**: GIVEN an upgrade with a `"creature"` requirement is `PURCHASABLE`, WHEN that creature is later removed from the menagerie by an unrelated system before the player confirms, THEN the upgrade reverts to `VISIBLE_LOCKED` and confirming is rejected (Edge Cases).
- **AC-6** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Rule 2a): GIVEN an upgrade with 2+ `requirements` entries where at least one is satisfied and at least one is not, WHEN `VISIBLE_LOCKED` is rendered, THEN each entry shows its own satisfied/unmet state and the specific target's resolved display name independently — not one collapsed pass/fail state for the whole upgrade.
- **AC-7**: GIVEN `upgrade_id` B's `prerequisite_upgrade_id` is `upgrade_id` A (same `target_structure_id`), WHEN A has not yet been `PURCHASED`, THEN B is not `PURCHASABLE` regardless of B's own `requirements`/cost state.
- **AC-8** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Rule 6a): GIVEN an upgrade transitions from `VISIBLE_LOCKED` to `PURCHASABLE` (its last unmet requirement or cost becomes satisfied), WHEN this happens while the player is not viewing the Building Upgrade tab, THEN a Sanctuary UI Connection Badge appears on the Equipment Forge tab (`sanctuary-ui.md` Core Rule 2) without requiring the player to have opened the tab.
- **AC-9** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Rule 6a): GIVEN a successful purchase per AC-3, WHEN it resolves, THEN the confirmatory chime plays exactly once, independent of whether `structure_upgraded`'s consuming system exists yet.
- **AC-10** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Edge Cases): GIVEN a `requirements` entry references an unrecognized `structure_id`/`item_id`/`card_id`/`species_id`, WHEN checked, THEN the entry resolves `false` and the upgrade never reaches `PURCHASABLE` via that entry.
- **AC-11** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Edge Cases): GIVEN two upgrades (same or different `target_structure_id`) share an identical `requirements` entry, WHEN one is `PURCHASED`, THEN the shared requirement remains independently satisfied and unaffected for the other upgrade's own evaluation.
- **AC-12** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Rule 1b): GIVEN a `target_structure_id`'s `prerequisite_upgrade_id` chain forms a cycle, WHEN content is validated at load time, THEN `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate()` rejects it as a content error, never reaching a runtime deadlock.
- **AC-13** (added 2026-08-12 design review, Rule 1a/1b): GIVEN a `target_structure_id`'s `prerequisite_upgrade_id` chain exceeds 3 upgrades, WHEN content is validated at load time, THEN `BuildingUpgradeResource.validate()` rejects it as a content error, never reaching an undefined `upgrade_tier_multiplier` lookup at runtime.
