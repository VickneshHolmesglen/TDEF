## Overview

Sanctuary UI is the presentation shell that makes the Sanctuary's five gameplay systems — the fused Inventory+Crafting two-panel screen, Menagerie, Defense Building, and Equipment Forge — navigable and visible to the player, plus a full-frame overview state where built structures and creature habitats are shown whenever no work-room is open. Players interact with it directly and constantly: it is the only way to open Crafting, browse the tech tree, tend the menagerie, or imbue equipment between missions. Without it, the five systems it wraps — all already implemented at the gameplay-logic level — have no player-facing surface at all; the meta-progression loop the game concept describes does not currently exist in a form a player can touch. This GDD is the first document to define how these five independently-designed screens relate to each other as a single coherent *navigation shell*, since none of their own GDDs assume a unifying shell exists — `defense-building.md` already assumed the overview *rendering surface itself* exists (see the Interactions table note below), so this document's contribution is the shell around that surface, not the surface's original invention.

## Player Fantasy

**The Curator of Your Own Cabinet**

Between missions, the player is not managing menus — they are tending a hand-carved cabinet of wonders that is unmistakably theirs. Every screen is a drawer pulled open on the same object: the towers they favor, the creatures they've bred, the runes they've imbued. Moving between Crafting, Forge, Menagerie, and Defense Building isn't meant to feel like navigating separate apps — it's turning the same object in their hands. **Revision note**: the original draft promised this legibility at the slot level ("watching a choice in one drawer light up the connected slots in another") while also requiring rooms to be mutually exclusive — a contradiction the design review caught. The mechanism that actually delivers this now is smaller and tab-strip-scoped (Core Rule 2's Connection Badges, Core Rule 9): a choice made in one room lights up the *tab* of the room it connects to, which is always visible, rather than lighting up a slot inside a room that isn't currently on screen. The answer to "what happens when these two things interact?" arrives as a legible nudge toward where to look next, not as two full panels open side by side.

The peak moment is the step-back: the player stops adjusting and simply looks, and sees a sanctuary no other player could have built, because it is the physical record of how they think. This is the-base-as-statement — not a progress bar filling, but a self-portrait taking shape, one drawer at a time. This moment is reachable both automatically (every post-mission return, Core Rule 5) and voluntarily (the `Overview` tab is one press away from anywhere, Core Rule 5's revision) — the original draft only supported the forced version.

**On the Loadout tab (added 2026-07-24, integrated 2026-07-24, register corrected in a same-day follow-up pass)**: the cabinet has one drawer that doesn't hold an object at all — it holds the question of how the player intends to face the next fight, both who they'll bring (Hero Skill Build) and how hard they've chosen to make it (Tarot). That question deserves its own full weight, which is exactly why it lives at Tarot Deck UI's own desk rather than being crammed into this cabinet alongside them (see `tarot-deck-ui.md`'s own Player Fantasy, "Signing Both Halves of a Prediction," for that desk's own full framing). **Correction, closing a gap a fresh review found**: this section previously called reaching that desk "consequential" — the wrong word, since it invited comparison to Launch/Depart's genuinely consequential, irreversible session-ending action (Core Rule 8, Core Rule 10's own reachability principle). Reaching the desk costs the same one input as opening any other drawer, and is exactly as reversible — the weight belongs entirely to what's *decided* once there, not to the act of walking over. This shell's only job is to make that walk feel like one continuous motion rather than a separate errand — an intentional design goal for the transition treatment (Visual/Audio Requirements), not yet a claim this document has verified a real player actually feels (see the Experiential Criterion).

## Detailed Design

> **Revision note (2026-07-24, post `/design-review`)**: The original Core Rules 1/4/9 modeled the shell as "diorama always visible in a margin + a small docked room panel," which the design review (`design/gdd/reviews/sanctuary-ui-review-log.md`) found to be undersized on two independent axes at once: the reserved margin (18%/10%) was too thin to keep the diorama legible, and the 4 rooms' own already-authored full-rect screens (confirmed against `InventoryScreen.tscn`'s actual anchor/container structure) don't reflow into what would have been left. Worse, forbidding cross-room visibility (old Core Rule 9) directly contradicted the Player Fantasy's own central promise. The model below replaces "always-partially-visible diorama + squeezed room" with "diorama owns `OVERVIEW` fully, rooms own the full working frame, cross-system legibility is delivered through a always-visible **Connection Badge** layer on the tab strip instead of simultaneous panel visibility." This is a smaller, more honest promise that the shell can actually keep.

### Core Rules

**Core Rule 1 — Structural Model: Full-Frame Diorama, Full-Frame Rooms, Persistent Chrome**
Sanctuary UI is a single shell built from two mutually-exclusive full-frame content states plus one always-present chrome layer:
1. **The Sanctuary-overview** — a full-canvas diorama (same oblique-camera convention as every other Sanctuary scene) showing all built structures and habitat occupancy at their fixed, hand-authored positions. Rendered at full frame size (minus chrome, Rule below) only while `OVERVIEW` is the active state. It keeps running (subscribed to `sanctuary_structure_built` etc., per the Edge Cases terminal-latch note) even while occluded by a room, but is not required to be visually on-screen during a `ROOM_*` state — that requirement is what made the prior model unbuildable.
2. **A room panel** — when a work-room (Inventory+Crafting, Menagerie, Defense Building, or Equipment Forge) is active, it is hosted at full frame size (minus chrome) inside a shell-owned `RoomHost` `Control`, anchored full-rect. This is the same order of magnitude as how each room's own screen was already authored (`InventoryScreen.tscn` is itself a full-rect `Control` containing an `HBoxContainer`/`GridContainer` layout) — the shell reserves only the tab strip's width and the header's height, not an additional always-visible diorama margin on top of that.
3. **Shell chrome** — the Room Tab Strip (with its Connection Badges, Core Rule 2) and the persistent resource header (Core Rule 3), both always rendered regardless of which state is active, and both genuinely thin (tab strip ≤96px wide, header ≤64px tall — see Tuning Knobs) rather than a percentage-of-frame margin. **Z-order (closes a gap an independent re-verification pass found)**: shell chrome is always drawn on a layer above `RoomHost`'s content — a hosted room introducing its own `CanvasLayer` (none currently do; verified against every room `.tscn` that exists today, see OQ-5) would otherwise be able to render over the tab strip/header despite Rule 1's "always rendered" promise. This is an explicit ordering guarantee, not an assumption left implicit.

**Core Rule 2 — Navigation: Persistent 7-Entry Tab Strip + Connection Badges** *(revised 2026-08-31 — extended from 6 to 7 entries, see Core Rule 11)*
A fixed vertical tab strip (left edge of the screen) lists **7 entries** — **Overview · Inventory & Crafting · Menagerie · Defense Building · Equipment Forge · Skills · Loadout** — icon + label, always visible. Six of these seven select a shell-internal state (`OVERVIEW` or a `ROOM_*` state, now including the new `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` at position 6, Core Rule 11); the seventh, **Loadout**, hands off to a different shell entirely (Core Rule 10) — this asymmetry is not new or special-cased, since `OVERVIEW` itself is already the tab strip's one entry that isn't a `ROOM_*` room; Loadout is simply the tab strip's second entry that isn't one either. *(Corrected 2026-08-31, second pass — this sentence previously said "five" and "the sixth," stale from before Skills was inserted at position 6, pushing Loadout to 7th.)* **Container architecture, stated explicitly (closes a Godot-implementation ambiguity a fresh review flagged)**: the tab strip is a `VBoxContainer` of independently fixed-size button/entry nodes, **not** a Godot `TabBar`/`TabContainer` — this matters because `TabBar`'s auto-fit/overflow behavior would introduce real sizing risk when the entry count changed (5→6→7), which a plain `VBoxContainer` of fixed-width children does not; each entry keeps its own configured width (Tuning Knobs) regardless of how many siblings exist. **Sizing at 7 entries**: the strip's height grows to accommodate the additional entry (it's a vertical list, so height is the flexible dimension, not width) — the per-entry width (Tuning Knobs, 56–96px) is unchanged and doesn't need re-deriving, since it was never divided across the total entry count to begin with.
- **K/M**: click any tab directly; numeric hotkeys `1`–`7` jump directly to any entry; `[`/`]` cycle prev/next.
- **Gamepad**: `LT`/`RT` cycle prev/next entry (all 7, including Loadout). Deliberately *not* `LB`/`RB` — those are already committed to in-room tab switching in 4 of the 6 wrapped systems (Inventory's category tabs, Defense Building's Tech/Build toggle, Equipment Forge's Craft/Imbue toggle, and now Skills' own Loadout/Tree toggle — Core Rule 11); reusing them for room-switching would make the same physical input mean different things depending on invisible state. The split is consistent everywhere: **triggers = switch tab-strip entries, bumpers = switch tabs within the open room.**
- **Onboarding**: the first time Sanctuary UI ever mounts (a project-level "has this player seen it before" flag, not persisted per Save & Persistence's "no UI state saved" rule — session-local only), a one-time non-blocking tooltip anchored to the tab strip states the trigger/bumper split in one line. Dismisses on any input. This closes the discoverability gap the review flagged — the split is designer-legible but not player-discoverable without it.
- **Connection Badge** (new mechanic, replaces the old "cross-room highlighting" idea and resolves the Player-Fantasy/Core-Rule-9 contradiction the review found): each of the 4 room tabs can show a small live-updating badge — an icon + optional count — driven purely by EventBus subscriptions the shell already needs for other reasons (e.g. `sanctuary_structure_built` incrementing Defense Building's badge; a new creature reaching READY state incrementing Menagerie's; a newly-unlocked recipe incrementing Crafting's). This is how "a choice in one drawer lights up the connected slots in another" is actually delivered: the tab strip is the one piece of chrome that IS always visible regardless of active room, so it is the correct place to surface cross-system legibility — not a simultaneous-panel-visibility requirement the shell can't structurally support. Badges are purely informational (no unread/dismiss state to persist, matching the no-persisted-UI-state rule) and clear themselves the instant their owning room is entered. The exact badge-triggering event list per room is a Tuning Knob (below), since it depends on which EventBus signals each room's own GDD already emits. **Loadout does not carry a Connection Badge** — it hands off to a separate shell with its own state, not a room this shell's own EventBus subscriptions have visibility into; a badge would require Sanctuary UI to reach into Tarot Deck UI's own data, which neither document's Dependencies claim.
- **Real gap this design still leaves open, flagged not hidden**: Connection Badges (this rule) tell the player "something changed in room X," but not specifically *which prior action of yours caused it* — a badge is a notification, not a causal trace. Whether that's a real problem for the Player Fantasy's "a choice... lights up" language, or an acceptable simplification, is tracked as Open Question OQ-7 rather than resolved here; it predates and is independent of this session's Loadout redesign.

**Core Rule 3 — Persistent Resource Header**
A read-only materials display renders at the top edge of the screen in every state, including `OVERVIEW`. It is a pure subscriber to `materials_earned`/`materials_spent` — it owns no data, validates nothing, and has no interactive affordance (it is not a 6th navigable region). Gold is explicitly not shown here — gold is mission-scoped ephemeral budget (`resource-economy.md` Rule 1, ADR-0018) with no Sanctuary-context meaning; it remains visible only in Mission HUD, where its lifecycle is actually correct. Each room's own cost-preview logic remains independently authoritative for affordability checks at the point of spend.

**Core Rule 4 — Menagerie's Habitat Renders As Its Own GDD Already Specifies**
The prior draft treated Menagerie's full-bleed habitat view as a conflict requiring a special camera-zoom patch onto the Sanctuary-overview. That conflict no longer exists: since Core Rule 1 no longer requires the diorama to stay visible during `ROOM_*` states, Menagerie's room hosts its own already-designed full-bleed habitat screen exactly as `menagerie.md` specifies, unmodified, the same way the other 3 rooms do. This removes an entire camera-tween/interrupt/live-position-retarget subsystem (and its associated Godot `Camera2D`-smoothing gotcha) that existed only to serve a constraint this revision removes. Resolves `OQ-1` from the original draft: `menagerie.md` needs no cross-reference note, since Sanctuary UI no longer alters how its habitat is presented.

**Core Rule 5 — Entry Always Defaults to `OVERVIEW`; Player-Initiated Return Is Also Supported**
Sanctuary UI is entered automatically whenever `mission_phase_changed` reaches `MISSION_COMPLETE` or `DEFEAT` and SceneManager transitions to the Sanctuary scene. Every entry defaults to the `OVERVIEW` tab, regardless of which room was open on a prior visit — consistent with `save-persistence.md`'s confirmed "no UI state is ever saved," and a deliberate pacing choice: every return trip opens with the Player Fantasy's "step back and look" beat before the player dives into a specific room. **Additionally** (closing a gap the design review flagged — the peak moment was previously forced-only, never player-initiated): the tab strip's own `Overview` entry (tab 1, hotkey `1`) is reachable from any `ROOM_*` state exactly like any other tab, so a player can voluntarily return to look at their sanctuary mid-session without that being a special mechanic — it's simply the existing navigation model, now explicitly named as satisfying this requirement.

**Core Rule 6 — Room Lifecycle: Destroy-and-Recreate on Every Switch; One `InputModeTracker` Per Navigable Region**
**Lifecycle (tightened 2026-07-24 — a fresh review found the original teardown sequence only gated input, not signal delivery, a gap 2 independent specialists caught from different angles)**: rooms are never kept alive-but-hidden. Each `ROOM_*` entry instantiates that room's scene fresh as a child of the shell-owned `RoomHost` `Control` (Core Rule 1); each exit (any of: Cancel-at-root, tab/hotkey/`LT`/`RT` switch, Launch/Depart, or Loadout selection per Core Rule 10) runs the outgoing room through **two synchronous cleanup steps, in order, before `queue_free()`-ing it**: (1) disconnect every EventBus subscription the room instance itself owns (every room hosted by this shell is required to expose this as a callable step — whatever each room's own GDD names it — since `queue_free()`'s end-of-frame deferral otherwise leaves the room's own signal callbacks live for the rest of the current frame, able to fire against a node that's about to be freed); (2) call `set_process_unhandled_input(false)` on it, closing the same-frame double-input-handling gap. This mirrors `mission_hud.gd`'s Planning Cluster precedent exactly (chosen there for the same reason: guaranteed `_ready()` re-fire, no stale subscription risk) and resolves two things the prior draft left ambiguous: (a) a room's nested state (e.g. Menagerie's breeding-pen flow) never survives a lateral switch — re-entering a room always resumes at that room's own root, never mid-flow, matching the "tolerate an unmount arriving before mount-complete" Edge Case's implicit assumption; (b) the "silently discarded staged state" Edge Case (Equipment Forge/Defense Building) is a direct, unavoidable consequence of this lifecycle, not a separately-implemented discard — nothing needs to explicitly clear staged state, because the node holding it no longer exists after the switch.

**Focus — seven `InputModeTracker` instances, each with a single, unambiguous owner (redesigned 2026-07-24, replacing a prior patch that retrofitted 2 OVERVIEW-content-zone buttons into this tracker to preserve this rule's own instance count — that retrofit is what triggered the 4th-pass freeze; this version doesn't need it, because Loadout is no longer a content-zone button, it's a tab, per Core Rule 10; count revised again 2026-08-31 when `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` was added, Core Rule 11)**: per ADR-0011's own stated scope ("instanced per interactive UI screen... extending to Sanctuary UI once designed"), each of the **5** work-rooms (4 original + `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS`) keeps its own `InputModeTracker` instance for its internal navigation graph (unchanged from each room's own GDD), freshly constructed on each room instantiation per the lifecycle above. The shell owns two additional, persistent instances: one scoped to the **7-entry tab strip's** own focus ring (Core Rule 2 — Overview, the 5 rooms, and Loadout are all ordinary focus-traversable entries in this one tracker, no special-casing for any of them), and one scoped to `OVERVIEW`'s own structure/habitat-selection focus ring (Core Rule 1's secondary entry path). **Seven instances total** (5 rooms + tab strip + Overview) — never seven *simultaneously live* trackers, since the active room's is the only one of the 5 that exists at any moment (0 or 1), plus the 2 persistent shell trackers; "seven total" describes the full roster across the shell's lifetime, not a concurrent count.
**Default focus in `OVERVIEW`**: the tab strip's `InputModeTracker` holds default focus whenever `OVERVIEW` becomes active. `OVERVIEW`'s own structure-selection tracker only gains focus when the player explicitly directs input at the diorama — a mouse click/hover on a structure, or (gamepad/keyboard) a dedicated "enter diorama" input (`Down` from the tab strip's bottom-most focused tab, mirroring how many list-to-grid UIs hand off focus at a boundary) — never as a second simultaneously-focused region. Cancel from the diorama's structure-selection ring returns focus to the tab strip, not to nothing. Launch/Depart (Core Rule 8) remains the shell's only OVERVIEW-content-zone action — its own focus-reachability was already scoped in `design/ux/sanctuary-ui.md` as an implementation-detail choice (reachable past the diorama's structure ring, or via a direct hop from the tab strip) and is unaffected by this redesign, since Loadout no longer shares a mechanism with it.

**Core Rule 7 — Back Navigation Reuses the Existing Cancel Idiom**
Pressing Cancel (`Esc` / gamepad East face button) inside a room's own nested state (e.g., mid-selection in Menagerie's breeding-pen flow, Equipment Forge's staged imbue confirmation) steps back one stage at a time, exactly as each room's own GDD already specifies — the active state remains that room's `ROOM_*` state throughout. Pressing Cancel again once the room has nothing left to back out of (its own root) exits the room entirely: the active state transitions to `OVERVIEW`, matching the States and Transitions table exactly, and focus naturally lands on the tab strip since no room panel is docked there (also triggers Core Rule 6's destroy-on-exit lifecycle). No separate "leave room" action exists.

**Core Rule 8 — Exit: Departure to Mission Select**
Sanctuary UI hands off to Mission Select via an explicit Launch/Depart action presented in `OVERVIEW` only. No event fires when switching between Sanctuary UI's own internal room states — those are local UI navigation. **Correction (a fresh-context review found this claim went stale the moment Core Rule 10 added a second one)**: Launch/Depart is no longer the *only* externally-visible transition this GDD owns — Core Rule 10's Loadout hand-off is a second, structurally identical one. Both share the property that internal navigation is silent and only a genuine hand-off is observable.

**Core Rule 10 — Loadout: The Tab Strip's 7th Entry (was 6th, until Core Rule 11 added Skills 2026-08-31), Hand-off to Tarot Deck UI (redesigned 2026-07-24; ordering/ownership details tightened in a same-day follow-up pass after a fresh full review found them underspecified)**
**Loadout is a normal tab-strip entry (Core Rule 2), not a special OVERVIEW-only content-zone button.** Selecting Loadout — via click, hotkey `6`, or `LT`/`RT` cycling, from **any** state (`OVERVIEW` or any `ROOM_*` — unlike Launch/Depart, see the reachability-principle note below) — triggers the following **explicit, ordered sequence** (added because a fresh review found the original prose and the terminal-latch rule contradicted each other on ordering, and 3 other specialists independently found the same gap from different angles):
1. **Kill any in-flight shell-level transition first** — the drawer-slide room-switch tween (Visual/Audio Requirements) is shell-owned (attached to `RoomHost`, never to the room instance itself — stated explicitly here to close a gap a fresh review found: an in-flight tween killed mid-flight can never null-ref against a room node, because it was never the room's own tween to begin with).
2. **Terminal latch fires**: the shared `_departing`-equivalent flag for this transition is set and every EventBus subscription **the shell itself owns** is synchronously disconnected — unchanged from Core Rule 8's own established discipline (Edge Cases).
3. **If a room is currently active, it is torn down**: this is where Core Rule 6's own lifecycle runs — but **extended** (fixing a real gap 2 specialists independently found): a room's own teardown must **synchronously disconnect every EventBus subscription the room instance itself owns**, not only call `set_process_unhandled_input(false)`. The prior wording only gated input, not signal delivery — a room's own callback could still fire during the one-frame `queue_free()` deferral window if the corresponding event happened to fire that same frame. Every room hosted by this shell is required to expose this as a synchronous cleanup step (whatever each room's own GDD calls it), called here before `queue_free()`, the same way `set_process_unhandled_input(false)` already is. This extended teardown sequence applies to **every** Core Rule 6 exit trigger (Cancel, tab/hotkey/`LT`/`RT`, Launch/Depart, Loadout) — not a Loadout-specific rule, a tightening of Core Rule 6 itself.
4. **`DEPARTING_TO_LOADOUT` is entered and the hand-off proceeds.**

**Reachability principle, stated explicitly (a fresh review found this asymmetry was real but unarticulated)**: Launch/Depart is deliberately `OVERVIEW`-only because it is *irreversible* — it ends the Sanctuary session (Core Rule 8's own "contemplative beat before a consequential action" reasoning). Loadout is reachable from anywhere because it is *reversible, lateral navigation* — selecting it doesn't end anything; the player returns to exactly where Sanctuary UI would otherwise be. Gating a reversible action behind a detour through `OVERVIEW` would only add friction to a decision the player has already made, with no corresponding safety benefit — the general principle this document now states plainly: **irreversible exits get a pacing gate; reversible lateral navigation does not.**

**Hand-off mechanism (named explicitly — a fresh review found the original text was less precise than its own stated model)**: the hand-off is a direct call to Tarot Deck UI's own established entry point, per `tarot-deck-ui.md`'s own Interactions table — the same tier of precision Core Rule 8 already uses for its own Mission Select hand-off (itself flagged, per Open Question OQ-2, as not yet classified under ADR-0020's Criterion A/B framework). Loadout's hand-off carries the identical flag, tracked as a new Open Question rather than silently assumed resolved.

Returning from Tarot Deck UI re-enters a fresh Sanctuary UI instance at `OVERVIEW`, identical to any other `scene_transition_completed(target_context="SANCTUARY")` entry (Core Rule 5) — Tarot Deck UI hands off back to Sanctuary UI, not directly to Mission Select, since a player leaving loadout editing has not launched anything.

**Connection Badge for Loadout — corrected framing (a fresh review found the original justification overstated)**: Loadout does not currently carry a Connection Badge, but the reason is a **deferred design choice**, not a hard architectural impossibility as the original text implied — `tarot-deck-ui.md`'s own Core Rule 7 (Current Build Snapshot) already proves cross-shell data visibility is achievable when deliberately designed. Building a Loadout badge would require a new, purpose-built EventBus signal expressing "something in your loadout is worth a second look," which is real, additional scope this redesign pass didn't take on — tracked as Open Question OQ-8, not asserted as impossible.

**Core Rule 11 — Skills: The Tab Strip's New 6th Entry, a `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` Room Hosting Both Hero Skill Build and Hero Skill Tree (new 2026-08-31)**
`/review-all-gdds` (2026-08-31) found `hero-skill-tree.md` named Sanctuary UI as an upstream dependency needing a mount point that did not exist anywhere in this document — and, independently, that `hero-skill-build.md` had never had a stated Sanctuary UI entry point either, despite being an Approved, implementation-ready system in its own right. This rule closes both gaps in one addition, since the two systems are causally linked (a Hero Skill Tree node purchase is what makes a skill available to slot in Hero Skill Build) and naturally belong in the same room.

**Skills is a normal `ROOM_*` tab-strip entry** (tab position 6, between Equipment Forge and Loadout — hotkey `6`; Loadout shifts to hotkey `7`), unlike Loadout's external hand-off (Core Rule 10) — it follows the same in-shell room model as Inventory & Crafting, Menagerie, Defense Building, and Equipment Forge (Core Rule 6's destroy-and-recreate lifecycle applies identically, including its own fresh `InputModeTracker` per Focus, above).

- **Internal sub-navigation via `LB`/`RB`** (the same bumper idiom already established by Inventory's category tabs, Defense Building's Tech/Build toggle, and Equipment Forge's Craft/Imbue toggle — Core Rule 2): two sub-screens, **Loadout** (Hero Skill Build's own slot A/B/C/D + Ultimate + passive screen, per `hero-skill-build.md`'s UI Requirements, unmodified) and **Skill Tree** (Hero Skill Tree's own 4-chain spend screen, per `hero-skill-tree.md`'s Dependencies — that document's own Visual/UI treatment remains deferred, OQ-3, but the *mount point and reachability* are resolved here regardless of when that content ships). Bumpers never trigger a tab-strip switch, matching every other room's existing bumper-vs-trigger discipline.
- **Default sub-screen on room entry**: Loadout (Hero Skill Build) — the more frequently-visited of the two (equipping an already-learned skill is a more common action than spending a freshly-earned point), matching this shell's general precedent of defaulting to the higher-frequency view (`OVERVIEW` itself being the highest-frequency view of all).
- **Connection Badge**: the Skills tab can show a Connection Badge (Core Rule 2) driven by `skill_unlocked` (a newly-learned, not-yet-equipped skill) or `skill_tree_point_earned`/`skill_tree_overflow_point_earned` (an unspent banked point) — both are exactly the kind of EventBus signal Core Rule 2's badge mechanic already exists for, and both are things this shell already needs to be aware of via other rules. The badge clears the instant `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` is entered, same as every other room's badge.
- **No new hand-off, no new external shell.** Both sub-screens' actual UI content remains entirely owned by their respective GDDs (`hero-skill-build.md`, `hero-skill-tree.md`) — this rule only resolves *where in Sanctuary UI's navigation graph* they live, not their internal layout.
- **Flagged 2026-09-01, partially resolved.** `hero-skill-tree.md`'s Choice-node redesign (Rule 9, Formula 2) means a purchase on the Skill Tree sub-screen is no longer a single confirm action — it now requires presenting the node's 2–3 `tier_skill_ids` options to the player and capturing which one they select (`player_selected_skill_id`) before the purchase can fire, and the sub-screen must render the resulting `LOCKED_OUT` siblings distinctly from ordinary locked nodes. **Both screens' content now exists** (`hero_skill_build_screen.gd`, `hero_skill_tree_screen.gd` — the latter implements exactly this multi-option-select + `LOCKED_OUT` treatment, live-verified via Godot MCP), but **this rule's own `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` room itself was never actually implemented in code** — real gap found 2026-09-01: `sanctuary_ui.gd`'s `ROOM_SCENES` dictionary (the actual room-mounting mechanism) has no `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` entry, no tab-strip 6th button, no LB/RB bumper toggle between the two screens. This rule describes a shell integration that was designed and Approved but never built — both screens are currently unreachable from the live game. Building the actual `ROOM_HERO_SKILLS` wiring (tab strip entry, bumper toggle, RoomHost mounting) is separate, unstarted work.

**Core Rule 9 — Cross-Room Highlighting Stays Confined to a Room; Cross-System Legibility Lives on the Tab Strip**
The existing focus-highlight-on-selection pattern (Crafting highlighting Inventory slots on recipe-select; Equipment Forge's Craft tab reusing it) remains scoped to screens where both sides are visible simultaneously within one room. It does not extend across rooms — selecting something in Menagerie never jump-navigates to or pre-highlights something in a different room; rooms are mutually exclusive content, so there is no simultaneous-visibility case for slot-level cross-highlighting to apply to. **This is a narrower rule than the original draft's** — it only forbids slot-level highlighting across rooms, not all cross-system legibility. That legibility is instead delivered by Core Rule 2's Connection Badges on the always-visible tab strip: a player who crafts a recipe that unlocks a new Menagerie interaction sees a badge light up on the Menagerie tab immediately, without needing both screens open at once. The Player Fantasy's "a choice in one drawer lights up the connected slots in another" is honored at the tab-strip granularity, not the slot granularity — a deliberate, smaller promise than the original draft implied, chosen because the slot-granularity version is structurally impossible under a mutually-exclusive-room model (per the design review's finding) without abandoning that model entirely.

## Formulas

None. Sanctuary UI is a pure presentation/navigation shell — every value it displays (the resource header, the Connection Badges) is a direct EventBus payload read or a simple counter increment/clear, and every behavior it defines (room switching, back-navigation) is state transition logic, not a derived calculation from multiple inputs. This matches `mission-hud.md`'s own precedent for the same reason. Any timing values are configurable constants, captured in Tuning Knobs below, not formulas.

## Edge Cases

- **If the player mashes `LT`/`RT` (or numeric hotkeys) faster than a room finishes mounting**: each input immediately tears down the in-flight room and mounts the next — no queueing. Per Core Rule 6's destroy-and-recreate lifecycle, the outgoing room has its own EventBus subscriptions disconnected and `set_process_unhandled_input(false)` called on it synchronously before `queue_free()`, so it cannot double-handle an input or a stray signal in the same frame the next room mounts (a Godot-specific `queue_free()`-deferral risk the design review flagged).
- **If Loadout is selected while a room's own drawer-slide mount/switch tween is still mid-flight** (a genuinely new combination a fresh review found wasn't covered by the LT/RT-vs-LT/RT case above, since Loadout is a second, different entry point into the same teardown path): the tween is killed immediately, exactly like any other interrupted room-switch tween — safe by construction, since Core Rule 10 now states explicitly that the drawer-slide tween is shell-owned (`RoomHost`'s own transition controller), never the room instance's own, so killing it can never null-ref against a room node that's mid-teardown.
- **If Cancel is pressed with focus already on the tab strip and no room open**: no-op — there is no "exit Sanctuary UI via Cancel" path; exit is exclusively Core Rule 8's Launch/Depart.
- **If Launch/Depart is pressed twice in rapid succession**: the second press is discarded. `DEPARTING` is entered and all further input is ignored the same frame the state flips, not a frame later.
- **If any room-selection input (tab, hotkey, `LT`/`RT`, or an Overview structure click) arrives while in `DEPARTING`**: discarded — covered by the terminal-latch rule below.
- **If `sanctuary_structure_built` arrives while any state other than `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` is active, including `OVERVIEW` itself**: the Sanctuary-overview's structure rendering updates immediately regardless of which room is currently active — the overview is shell-owned and keeps running for the shell's whole lifetime (Core Rule 1) and must keep listening at all times, not just while `OVERVIEW` is the active tab. It also increments Defense Building's Connection Badge (Core Rule 2) if `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` is not the active state.
- **If `materials_earned`/`materials_spent` haven't fired yet by the time Sanctuary UI finishes mounting** (boot-order race): the header shows whatever materials total has accumulated locally so far (0 if none) — no poll-based fallback exists or is needed, since no external system owns a queryable materials balance either (Inventory is the ledger of record, but exposing a live poll API from it is a separate, larger scope not entered into here).
- **If a structure with no corresponding room** (e.g., Watchtower, per `defense-building.md`) **is selected via Overview's secondary entry path**: no room-mount is attempted — an inert info tooltip names the structure, matching that structure's own "no gameplay effect" framing. The Sanctuary-overview still renders it at its fixed position regardless (rendering is never gated on room-mapping, only the click-to-enter path is).
- **If the player switches rooms via `LT`/`RT`/hotkey while Equipment Forge or Defense Building has an unconfirmed staged action pending**: the staged state is discarded — a direct consequence of Core Rule 6's destroy-and-recreate lifecycle (the node holding the staged state ceases to exist), not a separately-implemented discard. A brief, non-blocking toast ("Staged changes discarded") plays on the departing room's own toast/notification affordance if it has one, or a shell-level equivalent otherwise — a design-review-driven addition so the player isn't left silently guessing why a staged action vanished, without adding a blocking confirm-modal that would slow down the tab-switch flow `LT`/`RT` is meant to keep fast. Nothing has been spent yet at the staged (not-yet-confirmed) step, so no data/resource loss occurs, only lost UI progress.
- **If the player selects the Loadout tab while Equipment Forge or Defense Building has an unconfirmed staged action pending** (added — an independent verification pass on the Core Rule 10 redesign found this case wasn't explicitly enumerated, even though Core Rule 6's own lifecycle already implies it): identical outcome and identical toast requirement to the room-switch case immediately above — Loadout's teardown of the active room (Core Rule 10) goes through the exact same destroy-and-recreate lifecycle as any other lateral switch, so this is not a new mechanism, only a previously-unlisted trigger for an already-specified one.
- **If Sanctuary UI's own EventBus subscriptions receive events during `DEPARTING`** (`sanctuary_structure_built`, `resources_changed`, or any other): discarded — see the terminal-latch rule immediately below.
- **If the Loadout tab is selected twice in rapid succession** (or once while a room is mid-teardown from a different lateral switch): the second/overlapping trigger is discarded, identically to Launch/Depart's own double-press edge case — `DEPARTING_TO_LOADOUT` is entered once and all further input is ignored the same frame the state flips.
- **If any EventBus event arrives while `DEPARTING_TO_LOADOUT` is active**: discarded, covered by the same terminal-latch rule as `DEPARTING` (below) — the two transitional states share one discipline, not two.
- **Terminal latch, including its frame-ordering guarantee** (tightened per the design review's systems-designer finding that "the instant" was previously unstated as a real ordering guarantee): **both** the Launch/Depart confirm handler and the Loadout tab-selection handler (Core Rule 10) share one `_departing` flag and one disconnect routine — whichever handler fires first, its *first EventBus-touching or state-mutating statement* (**precision added — a fresh review found "first statement" read as contradicting Core Rule 10's own step-1 tween-kill, which necessarily runs before it; the guarantee this rule actually protects is ordering relative to EventBus mutations and the shared flag, not literal handler-line-one, and Core Rule 10's tween-kill is EventBus-inert — it touches no subscription and reads no shared state, so it can safely precede the latch without weakening what the latch exists to guarantee**), before any other EventBus-touching logic in that handler runs, sets the shared flag AND synchronously disconnects every EventBus subscription this instance owns (rather than only flag-checking inside each handler). This closes the race the review identified: a `CONNECT_DEFERRED` subscription already queued for later in the same frame cannot fire after this point because the connection itself is gone, not merely flag-gated — mirroring `mission_hud.gd`'s `_terminal` pattern exactly, but explicit about disconnect-not-just-flag being what makes the guarantee real. **Correction to an overstated claim an independent verification pass found**: an earlier draft of this note claimed Launch/Depart and Loadout require "genuinely different physical inputs" to trigger — false as stated: both ultimately confirm via the same button (`A`/South face/click) once focused, per `design/ux/sanctuary-ui.md`'s own binding table. What is actually true, and load-bearing: they require different *focus paths* to reach that confirm (Launch/Depart is a fixed content-zone button; Loadout is a tab-strip entry reached via `LT`/`RT`/hotkey/d-pad-to-strip), and the two paths cannot both hold focus simultaneously — but a player *could* still plausibly trigger both in near-simultaneous frames via, e.g., a queued mouse click on one and a queued gamepad confirm on the other. The shared flag exists precisely to close that real case, not a hypothetical one — the mitigation is correct, the prior justification for why it was "narrower" was not. No reset logic is needed: `SceneManager.transition_to_sanctuary()` always instantiates a fresh scene via `change_scene_to_packed()` (confirmed against the real shipped implementation), so a "next mount" of a stale, already-latched instance can never occur — each Sanctuary visit is structurally a brand-new instance, same guarantee Mission HUD already relies on.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**Room-switch transitions**: a literal drawer-slide, not a fade/crossfade — the outgoing room slides toward the left edge (toward the tab strip) while the incoming room slides in from the same edge, overlapping briefly, at full `RoomHost` frame size throughout. Direction is always horizontal, never vertical/diagonal — one consistent physical axis sells "the same object, different drawer." Timing: see Tuning Knobs.

**`sanctuary_structure_built` appearance**: the instant-appearance flourish itself is already fully specified in `defense-building.md`'s own Visual/Audio Requirements — not re-specified here. Sanctuary UI's own requirement: the flourish plays whenever `OVERVIEW` is the active state at the moment the event fires (the overview is only actually rendered then, under this revision's model); when a `ROOM_*` state is active instead, the flourish is skipped in favor of the Defense Building Connection Badge (Core Rule 2) — the badge is not a lesser fallback, it is the *primary* feedback channel while a room is open, since there is nothing to flourish on-screen at that moment.

**Connection Badges**: a small icon, optionally with a numeric count, overlaid on a tab strip entry's icon corner — reuses the existing filled-vs-outline / non-color-first convention (a distinct badge glyph shape, not a color-only dot, so it remains colorblind-legible per the same principle `defense-building.md`'s own built/unbuilt structure distinction already establishes). Appears with a brief scale-pop (matching the resource header's existing ~1.0→1.15→1.0 treatment) so a badge arriving while the player is looking at the tab strip reads as a live event, not just a static icon change. Clears immediately (no fade-out beat) the instant its room is entered — the player is now looking directly at the thing the badge pointed to, so a lingering clear-animation would only delay feedback.

**`DEPARTING` beat**: shell chrome (tab strip, resource header) fades out first and fastest; the diorama camera performs a slow, deliberate pull-back/settle — mirroring MISSION_COMPLETE's "exhale" register, never a hard zoom or loading-screen wipe. Roughly 60% chrome-fade+pull-back / 40% hold-on-settled-frame within the ~0.5s budget, so there's a perceptible "look" beat before hand-off — a second, smaller echo of the Player Fantasy's own "step back" peak moment. No interrupt-handling needed (terminal latch already discards all input during this state).

**Shell chrome visual language**: the tab strip and resource header deliberately do **not** match any individual room's own visual register (Menagerie's diegetic diorama, Equipment Forge's diegetic workbench, Defense Building's abstract tech-graph, Inventory's flat grid all already disagree with each other on purpose — matching any one would arbitrarily privilege it over the others). Instead, shell chrome reuses Mission HUD's own established screen-space language wholesale: chamfered-rectangle shape grammar, UI Chrome/Parchment/Accent palette, flat single-inset-shade iconography, same motion contract (snappy-in ≤120ms / eased-out settle 150–200ms, no overshoot). This is the same principle that produced Mission HUD's look in the first place (chrome is a frame, not a painting) — Sanctuary UI is simply the first system to need that property outside combat. Active-tab state reuses the existing filled-vs-outline actionable convention (never color-only). The shell's own `InputModeTracker` focus ring uses the identical UI Accent Cyan treatment ADR-0011 already specifies — no new color.

**Diorama base layer**: full stacked-sprite/cast-shadow treatment, rendered whenever `OVERVIEW` is the active state — this is where Principle 1 (Everything Has Weight and Depth) applies; it explicitly does not apply to the chrome, which gets flat/low-relief drop shadows (purely vertical offset) at most, never stacked-sprite shadows. This is the concrete boundary between "diorama" and "shell."

**Persistent resource header**: UI Chrome background / UI Parchment text — never world-saturation Sanctum Amber, even though gold/materials are thematically amber-adjacent in-world (this is the element most tempted to violate the world/screen boundary, so stated explicitly). Numeric changes reuse Inventory's existing scale-pop feedback (~1.0→1.15→1.0 over ~0.2s) rather than inventing a new treatment. Zero interactive affordance — never renders a focus ring or hover state, since it is explicitly not a 6th navigable region (Core Rule 3). The boot-order mount-time fallback renders the same static treatment with the polled value — no loading state.

**Audio**: no `design/audio/` bible exists yet for this project (matching `mission-hud.md`'s own already-flagged gap) — directional only. Room-switch and structure-appear beats will eventually want short, restrained confirmation cues matching the art bible's SANCTUARY "Contemplative" energy profile (not WAVE_ACTIVE's "Urgent" register); Connection Badge arrival wants a subtle, non-intrusive chime distinct from any room's own in-room feedback cues; `DEPARTING` wants a distinct "closing" cue that doesn't compete with Mission Select's own entry audio. Specific SFX selection deferred until audio direction exists.

## Acceptance Criteria

Validated by `qa-lead` before finalizing, matching `mission-hud.md`'s own established organization: by Core Rule group, then States/Transitions, then Cross-System, then load-bearing Edge Cases, with items not independently testable as GDD-level runtime criteria flagged separately at the end.

### Group 1 — Core Rule 1: Structural Model (Full-Frame Diorama, Full-Frame Rooms, Persistent Chrome)

- [ ] **GIVEN** Sanctuary UI has just mounted to `OVERVIEW`, **WHEN** any of the 4 rooms is entered, **THEN** the Sanctuary-overview diorama node itself remains present in the scene tree (shell-owned, never freed) even though it is not the visible content — confirmed by verifying its node reference is unchanged before and after the transition, not merely that no error occurred.
- [ ] **GIVEN** any `ROOM_*` state is active, **THEN** the active room's root `Control` bounding rect equals the shell's `RoomHost` rect exactly — frame size minus the configured tab strip width and resource header height (Tuning Knobs) — not a percentage-of-frame margin on top of that.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active, **THEN** the Sanctuary-overview diorama's rendered bounding rect equals the frame minus the same tab strip width and header height — i.e. the diorama gets the full working frame while it is the active content, not a thin margin.
- [ ] **GIVEN** any state (`OVERVIEW` or any `ROOM_*`), **THEN** the Room Tab Strip and the persistent resource header are both present and rendering — never absent regardless of active state.

### Group 2 — Core Rule 2: Navigation (Persistent 7-Entry Tab Strip)

- [ ] **GIVEN** `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` is active, **WHEN** gamepad `LB`/`RB` is pressed, **THEN** it toggles that room's own internal Tech/Build tab (per `defense-building.md`) and the Sanctuary UI state does **not** change — confirming bumpers never trigger a tab-strip switch.

- [ ] **GIVEN** a Sanctuary UI instance has never mounted before this session, **WHEN** it mounts for the first time, **THEN** the tab-strip onboarding tooltip is shown; **GIVEN** it has already mounted once this session, **WHEN** it mounts again, **THEN** the tooltip is not shown again.

- [ ] **GIVEN** `ROOM_MENAGERIE` has nested state (e.g. mid-breeding-pen-flow), **WHEN** the player switches to `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` via hotkey and then back to `ROOM_MENAGERIE`, **THEN** Menagerie mounts fresh at its own root state, not resumed mid-flow — confirming the destroy-and-recreate lifecycle applies uniformly, not just to focus.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` becomes active, **THEN** the tab strip's `InputModeTracker` holds default focus (not `OVERVIEW`'s own structure-selection tracker) unless the player has already explicitly directed input at the diorama this state-entry.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `ROOM_MENAGERIE`'s `InputModeTracker` had focus on a breeding-pen element, **WHEN** the player switches to `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` via hotkey, **THEN** Equipment Forge's own `InputModeTracker` starts with its own independent default focus — Menagerie's focus state is not shared or carried over (and cannot be, since the Menagerie instance no longer exists per the lifecycle above).

- [ ] **GIVEN** the player switches among `OVERVIEW` and all 4 `ROOM_*` states repeatedly, **THEN** no externally-observable event fires on any of those internal transitions — confirmed by asserting no EventBus emission is captured except the eventual Launch/Depart **or Loadout** hand-off call. **(Corrected — the original AC only excluded Launch/Depart, stale the moment Core Rule 10 added a second externally-visible transition.)**

- [ ] **GIVEN** any state (`OVERVIEW` or any `ROOM_*`), **WHEN** the Loadout tab is selected, **THEN** the active state becomes `DEPARTING_TO_LOADOUT`, any previously-active room instance is torn down per Core Rule 6's own lifecycle first, and Sanctuary UI subsequently hands off to Tarot Deck UI — confirmed as a genuinely distinct destination from Launch/Depart's Mission Select hand-off, not a re-use of the same call.
- [ ] **GIVEN** a room with its own EventBus subscriptions and nested state is active, **WHEN** Loadout is selected, **THEN** the room's own subscriptions are disconnected and `set_process_unhandled_input(false)` is called on it, both confirmed complete (via call-order inspection) *before* `DEPARTING_TO_LOADOUT` is entered — mirroring Group 6's existing call-order AC for every other Core Rule 6 exit trigger, extended here to Loadout. **(Added — a fresh review found this exact rigor, already applied to every other exit trigger, had never been extended to Loadout despite Core Rule 6 explicitly listing it as one.)**

- [ ] **GIVEN** the player returns from Tarot Deck UI, **THEN** a fresh Sanctuary UI instance mounts at `OVERVIEW` — confirmed as the identical entry path Core Rule 5 already defines for any `scene_transition_completed(target_context="SANCTUARY")` arrival, not a special-cased "return from Loadout" path.

- [ ] **GIVEN** an item is selected in `ROOM_MENAGERIE` triggering its own in-room highlight, **WHEN** the player switches to `ROOM_INVENTORY_CRAFTING`, **THEN** no slot or element in Inventory+Crafting shows a highlight or pre-selected state as a result of the prior Menagerie selection.

- [ ] **GIVEN** a Connection-Badge-triggering event fires for a room other than the currently active one (e.g. `recipe_unlocked` while `ROOM_MENAGERIE` is active), **THEN** the corresponding tab shows a badge without any element inside the currently active room changing — confirming cross-system legibility is delivered at the tab-strip level, never by reaching into the active room's own content.

### States and Transitions

- [ ] **GIVEN** Sanctuary UI has just mounted, **THEN** the active state is `OVERVIEW` before any other input is processed (ties to Group 5).
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active, **WHEN** a structure/habitat is clicked directly on the diorama (secondary entry path) that maps to a room, **THEN** the active state becomes that room's `ROOM_*` state.
- [ ] **GIVEN** each of `ROOM_INVENTORY_CRAFTING`, `ROOM_MENAGERIE`, `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING`, `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` in turn, **WHEN** entered via tab/hotkey/`LT`/`RT`, **THEN** that room's own existing acceptance-criteria test suite (from its own GDD) passes unmodified when run inside Sanctuary UI's `RoomHost` — a regression check, not a re-assertion of behavior this GDD doesn't own.
- [ ] **GIVEN** any `ROOM_*` state, **WHEN** a different tab/hotkey/`LT`/`RT` input targets another room, **THEN** the active state changes directly to that room (no transient `OVERVIEW` pass-through state is observed), and the previously active room's instance is freed per Core Rule 6's lifecycle.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `DEPARTING` is active, **THEN** no action is available to the player (Launch/Depart, tabs, hotkeys, Cancel all produce no state change) — confirmed as a pure transitional state.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `DEPARTING` completes its transition beat, **THEN** the hand-off reaches Mission Select's `BROWSING` state (verified via Mission Select's own state, not just "an event fired").

### Cross-System Interaction Criteria

- [ ] **GIVEN** `sanctuary_structure_built` fires while `ROOM_MENAGERIE` is active, **THEN** the Sanctuary-overview's structure rendering updates to include the new structure at its fixed position — verified by then switching to `OVERVIEW` and observing the structure already present (not newly appearing on switch).
- [ ] **GIVEN** the same event fires while `OVERVIEW` itself is active, **THEN** the structure appears without requiring a state transition — confirming the "always listening, not gated on active tab" requirement (Interactions table + Edge Cases) actually holds for the one state where it would be trivially masked by re-render-on-entry.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active, **WHEN** Launch/Depart is confirmed and `DEPARTING` completes, **THEN** control genuinely passes to Mission Select (its scene/state becomes active and receives input) — not merely that Sanctuary UI's own state reads `DEPARTING` with no further observable effect.
- [ ] **GIVEN** a fresh Sanctuary UI instance is inspected at mount, **THEN** it has zero `SaveRestorer` registrations — confirming the "owns zero persisted state" dependency claim against Save & Persistence is actually true at runtime, not just asserted in the doc.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `scene_transition_completed(target_context="SANCTUARY")` fires from SceneManager, **THEN** Sanctuary UI mounts/resets to `OVERVIEW` as its sole trigger — confirmed by verifying no other event or direct call causes a (re)mount.

### Load-Bearing Edge Cases

- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active, **WHEN** the player presses `LT`/`RT`/hotkeys for 5 different rooms in rapid succession, faster than any single room's mount-complete callback fires, **THEN** only the last-targeted room ends up mounted and active, no errors are thrown, and no stale `InputModeTracker` signal connections remain from the torn-down intermediate rooms (verified via connection-count inspection after settling).
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active with focus on the tab strip and no room open, **WHEN** Cancel is pressed, **THEN** no state change, error, or side effect occurs — confirming Cancel is a genuine no-op here, not silently mapped to some other action (previously-uncovered Edge Case, added by the design review).
- [ ] **GIVEN** the player switches away from `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` or `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` via `LT`/`RT`/hotkey while an unconfirmed staged action is pending, **THEN** the staged action's data is gone after the switch (consistent with Core Rule 6's destroy-and-recreate lifecycle) AND a non-blocking "Staged changes discarded" toast is shown — verified as two separate assertions, since the review found the toast requirement had zero test coverage in the original draft.
- [ ] **GIVEN** the player selects the Loadout tab from `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` or `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` while an unconfirmed staged action is pending, **THEN** the same two assertions hold (staged data gone, toast shown) — added per an independent verification pass on the Core Rule 10 redesign, which found this trigger path was implied by Core Rule 6 but never explicitly tested.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `OVERVIEW` is active, **WHEN** Launch/Depart is pressed twice within the same frame (simulated double-input), **THEN** only one `DEPARTING` entry occurs and the second press produces no additional effect, no error, and no duplicate hand-off call.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `DEPARTING` is active, **WHEN** `sanctuary_structure_built`, `resources_changed`, or a room-selection input arrives, **THEN** all are discarded with no mutation to any rendered element (terminal latch) — verified across all three event types, not just one. **AND GIVEN** an EventBus subscription using `CONNECT_DEFERRED` was already queued before Launch/Depart was confirmed, **THEN** it still does not fire after `_departing` is set — confirming the disconnect-not-just-flag guarantee (revised Edge Cases terminal-latch note) actually closes the deferred-call race.
- [ ] **GIVEN** `sanctuary_structure_built` fires while `ROOM_DEFENSE_BUILDING` is **not** the active state (test against each of `OVERVIEW`, `ROOM_INVENTORY_CRAFTING`, `ROOM_MENAGERIE`, `ROOM_EQUIPMENT_FORGE` individually), **THEN** the overview's structure rendering updates in every case AND the Defense Building tab's Connection Badge appears in the 3 room-active cases — confirming both the "always listening" claim and the badge fallback hold for all 4 non-Defense-Building states, not just spot-checked once.
- [ ] **GIVEN** a structure with no corresponding room (e.g. Watchtower) is clicked via `OVERVIEW`'s secondary entry path, **THEN** no room-mount is attempted (active state remains `OVERVIEW`), an inert tooltip naming the structure appears, and the structure itself continues rendering at its fixed diorama position both before and after the click.
- [ ] **GIVEN** Sanctuary UI is mounting and `resources_changed` has not yet fired from Resource Economy, **THEN** the header displays Resource Economy's current balance via a direct mount-time poll rather than an empty/loading/zero placeholder state.

### Not Independently Testable as GDD-Level Runtime Criteria

Matching `mission-hud.md`'s own precedent, the following are excluded from the automated/manual-assertion list above and flagged instead:

- **Visual/Audio Requirements section in full** (drawer-slide "feel," `DEPARTING`'s 60/40 chrome-fade/hold pacing, shell chrome matching Mission HUD's visual language, diorama stacked-sprite vs. flat-chrome shadow treatment, Connection Badge scale-pop feel, all audio direction) — subjective visual/feel judgments requiring lead sign-off via screenshot/playtest evidence (Visual/Feel evidence tier), not black-box runtime assertions. Deferred to `design/ux/sanctuary-ui.md`'s own AC section once authored.
- **Tuning Knob "Too High"/"Too Low" descriptors** ("feels sluggish," "eats into room panel space," "badge fatigue") — guide manual tuning only; not independently verifiable thresholds.
- **Player Fantasy's "step back and look... self-portrait taking shape" claim** — requires a moderated playtest with player debrief; see the Experiential Criterion below.
- **Whether Connection Badges actually deliver felt cross-system legibility** (as opposed to their mechanical firing, which Groups 2/9 and the Load-Bearing Edge Cases above do test) — this is a design-intent question ("does the player notice and understand the connection"), not a runtime assertion; folded into the Experiential Criterion below rather than given a separate one, since it's the same category of claim.

### Experiential Criterion

- [ ] **GIVEN** a player completes a mission and returns to Sanctuary UI's `OVERVIEW`, **WHEN** debriefed afterward, **THEN** the player can articulate at least one specific choice their sanctuary visibly reflects (a structure, a habitat, a build direction) without being prompted for it — validated via moderated playtest, matching `mission-hud.md`'s own Experiential Criterion precedent. Not mechanically testable; tracked here so it isn't lost.
- [ ] **GIVEN** a player selects Loadout from mid-room (not from `OVERVIEW`), **WHEN** debriefed afterward, **THEN** they describe reaching Tarot Deck UI as a natural continuation of what they were doing, not a separate menu or errand — the direct experiential test of the Player Fantasy's "one continuous motion" claim. **(Added — a fresh review found this specific claim, unlike every comparable one elsewhere in this document, had been left with zero test coverage, neither here nor in the Not Independently Testable list above.)**
- [ ] **GIVEN** a Connection Badge appears on a tab while the player is in a different room, **WHEN** debriefed afterward, **THEN** the player can correctly say what the badge was about without opening that room first (i.e. the icon/context is legible enough to convey "something changed over there and here's roughly what," not just "something changed") — validated via moderated playtest. This is the direct experiential test of whether Core Rule 9's tab-strip-level legibility promise (the smaller, revised version of the original Player Fantasy claim) actually lands with a real player, not just that the badge mechanically fires.
